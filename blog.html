Cyber Blog

Practical reads to spot scams early.

Short, plain-language guides on the scams circulating across Assam right now.

Phishing

Five signs a "bank" message is fake

Phishing messages pretending to be from your bank are one of the most common scams in Assam right now. They usually arrive by SMS or WhatsApp, warning that your account will be blocked unless you act immediately. Here's how to tell the difference between a real bank alert and a scam.

  • It asks for your PIN, OTP, or CVV. No genuine bank — public or private — will ever ask for these over SMS, call, or WhatsApp.
  • It creates urgency. Phrases like "act within 2 hours" or "account will be suspended today" are designed to make you panic instead of think.
  • The link looks almost right, but not quite. Watch for extra letters, wrong domain endings, or shortened links that hide the real address.
  • It comes from a personal number, not an official shortcode. Most real bank alerts come from a registered sender ID, not a regular 10-digit mobile number.
  • The tone is slightly off. Spelling mistakes, awkward phrasing, or generic greetings ("Dear Customer") are common giveaways.

If you ever get a message like this, don't click the link. Call your bank directly using the number printed on your card or their official website — never the number given in the message itself.

Job Scams

The "work from home" trap

Job scams have grown fast across Assam, especially ones promising easy income for simple tasks like "liking videos" or "rating products online." These schemes usually follow a familiar pattern designed to build trust before asking for money.

  • The first few tasks pay out. Small, genuine-looking payments are used to build confidence before the real ask begins.
  • You're asked to pay to "unlock" bigger tasks. Legitimate jobs never require the employee to pay the employer.
  • Recruitment happens entirely over chat. No interview, no company website, no official email — just a Telegram or WhatsApp group.
  • There's constant pressure to recruit others. If the "job" depends on bringing in more people, it's a pyramid structure, not employment.

If you're offered work like this, take a step back before sending any money. A genuine employer will never ask you to pay first, and a quick search of the company name alongside the word "scam" often surfaces other people's experiences.

Digital Hygiene

Securing a shared family phone

Many households in Assam share a single smartphone across parents, grandparents, and children. A few simple settings changes can make that shared phone significantly safer for everyone who uses it.

  • Turn on app lock for banking and payment apps. This adds a second layer even if the phone itself is unlocked.
  • Disable "install from unknown sources." This stops scam APK files sent over WhatsApp from installing silently.
  • Review app permissions occasionally. Random apps requesting access to SMS or contacts is a common precursor to fraud.
  • Set up screen lock with a PIN, not a pattern. Patterns are easier to guess by watching finger movement.
  • Talk through one scam example together as a family. A five-minute conversation about a real scam message is often more effective than any app setting.

Digital in a shared household works best as a habit, not a one-time fix — revisiting these basics every few months keeps everyone alert to new tactics.
